# Evaluating a Company Before Investing

## Understand the business
Before investing in a company, make sure you understand how it makes money. Ask what products or services it sells, who its customers are, and whether demand is likely to last. If you cannot explain the business in a few sentences, you may not know enough to judge its risks. Reading the company's annual report is a good starting point.

## Check financial health
Review revenue and profit trends over several years rather than a single quarter. Look at profit margins, free cash flow and the level of debt. A company that is growing sales while keeping debt manageable and generating cash is generally in better shape than one that depends on constant borrowing. Compare these numbers with competitors in the same industry.

## Consider the valuation
A great company can still be a poor investment if the price paid is too high. Compare valuation ratios, such as the price-to-earnings ratio, with the company's historical levels and with its peers. A high ratio means investors expect strong growth, so there is less room for disappointment if growth slows.

## Competitive position and management
Consider whether the company has a durable advantage, such as a strong brand, technology, scale or customer loyalty, that helps it fend off competitors. Evaluate management's track record, how it allocates capital and whether its interests are aligned with shareholders. Honest communication in company reports is a positive sign.

## Industry and economic conditions
Every company operates within an industry and an economy. Consider industry growth, regulation, technological change and how sensitive the business is to economic cycles. Some businesses, such as consumer staples and utilities, tend to be steadier in downturns, while others, such as travel and luxury goods, tend to be more cyclical.

## Identify the key risks
Write down what could go wrong: loss of major customers, new competitors, legal or regulatory problems, rising costs or heavy debt. Knowing the main risks in advance helps you decide whether the potential reward justifies them and what developments would make you reconsider.

## Fit with your portfolio and goals
Think about how the investment fits your overall portfolio. Consider how much of your money would be in this one company and its sector, whether it matches your time horizon and risk tolerance, and whether it adds diversification or increases concentration. This information is educational and is not personalized financial advice.
