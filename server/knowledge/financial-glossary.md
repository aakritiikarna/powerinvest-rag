# Financial Glossary

## P/E ratio (price-to-earnings ratio)
The P/E ratio equals the current share price divided by the company's earnings per share (EPS). It shows how much investors are willing to pay for each dollar of the company's annual profit. For example, a stock priced at 100 with earnings per share of 5 has a P/E ratio of 20. A trailing P/E uses past earnings, while a forward P/E uses expected future earnings. A high P/E can mean investors expect strong growth, or that the stock is expensive; a low P/E can mean the stock is a bargain, or that the market expects trouble. The ratio is most meaningful when compared with similar companies and is not useful when earnings are negative.

## EPS (earnings per share)
Earnings per share is a company's net profit divided by its number of outstanding shares. It shows how much profit is attributable to each share and is used in valuation ratios such as the P/E ratio. Rising EPS over time is generally viewed as a positive sign.

## Dividend yield
Dividend yield is the annual dividend per share divided by the share price, expressed as a percentage. It shows the income an investor receives relative to the price paid. A very high yield can sometimes signal that the share price has dropped because the market doubts the dividend will continue.

## Market capitalization
Market capitalization is the share price multiplied by the number of shares outstanding. It measures the total market value of a company's equity and is used to classify companies as large-cap, mid-cap or small-cap.

## Volatility
Volatility is the degree to which an asset's price rises and falls over time. A highly volatile stock has larger and more frequent price swings, which usually means higher risk but also the potential for larger moves in either direction.

## Beta
Beta measures how much a stock tends to move compared with the overall market. A beta of 1 means it has moved in line with the market, above 1 means it has tended to move more, and below 1 means it has tended to move less. Beta is calculated from past data.

## ETF (exchange-traded fund)
An ETF is an investment fund that holds a basket of assets, such as stocks or bonds, and trades on an exchange like a single stock. Many ETFs track an index and offer low-cost diversification.

## Asset allocation
Asset allocation is how an investor divides a portfolio among asset classes such as stocks, bonds and cash. It is a major driver of both a portfolio's risk and its long-term return.

## IPO (initial public offering)
An IPO is the first time a private company offers its shares for sale to the public on a stock exchange. It allows the company to raise money and lets the public become shareholders.

## Blue-chip stock
A blue-chip stock is a share in a large, well-established and financially sound company with a long record of stable operations. Blue-chip stocks are generally less risky than smaller companies, but they are not risk-free.
