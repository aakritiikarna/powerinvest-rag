# Technical Analysis

## What technical analysis is
Technical analysis is a method of evaluating a stock by studying past price movements and trading volume, usually on charts, in order to identify patterns and possible future price direction. It assumes that price already reflects available information and that patterns tend to repeat. It is commonly used for shorter-term trading decisions about timing.

## Trends, support and resistance
A trend is the general direction of price: upward, downward or sideways. Support is a price level where buying interest has tended to stop a decline, and resistance is a level where selling has tended to stop a rise. Analysts watch for prices bouncing off or breaking through these levels.

## Common indicators
A moving average smooths prices over a chosen number of days to show the underlying trend. The relative strength index (RSI) measures the speed of recent price changes on a scale of 0 to 100 and is often used to flag conditions described as overbought or oversold. Volume shows how many shares traded and can indicate how strong a price move is. Indicators are calculated from past data and are not predictions.

## Fundamental versus technical analysis
Fundamental analysis asks what a company is worth by examining its business, financial statements, growth and competitive position, and is typically used to decide which companies to own over the long term. Technical analysis asks what the price is likely to do next by examining charts, trends and volume, and is typically used to decide when to buy or sell. Fundamental analysis focuses on value; technical analysis focuses on price behavior. Some investors use only one approach, while others combine them, using fundamentals to select a company and technicals to help with timing.

## Limits of technical analysis
Chart patterns can be interpreted differently by different people, and signals often fail or conflict. Critics argue that past prices cannot reliably predict future prices. Because many traders watch the same signals, results can also be hard to repeat. Technical analysis is best treated as one tool among several, not as a guarantee.
