# Stock Market Fundamentals

## What a stock is
A stock (also called a share or equity) represents a small slice of ownership in a company. When you hold a stock, you own part of the business and may benefit if its value rises or if it pays out part of its profits as dividends. Unlike a loan, a stock gives no guaranteed return, and its price can fall as well as rise.

## How stock exchanges work
A stock exchange, such as the New York Stock Exchange or Nasdaq, is a regulated marketplace where buyers and sellers trade shares. Prices move continuously during trading hours based on supply and demand. When more investors want to buy a stock than sell it, the price tends to rise; when more want to sell, it tends to fall. Most individual investors trade through a brokerage account rather than directly on the exchange.

## Market capitalization
Market capitalization, or market cap, is the total market value of a company's shares. It is calculated by multiplying the current share price by the number of shares outstanding. Companies are often grouped as large-cap, mid-cap or small-cap. Large-cap companies are generally more established and less volatile, while small-cap companies may offer more growth potential but usually carry more risk.

## Stock indexes
A stock index tracks the performance of a selected group of stocks and is used as a benchmark for the broader market. Examples include the S&P 500, which follows 500 large US companies, and the Nasdaq Composite. Investors compare their portfolio returns against an index to judge whether they are doing better or worse than the market as a whole.

## Bull and bear markets
A bull market is a sustained period of rising prices, usually driven by optimism and economic growth. A bear market is commonly defined as a decline of 20 percent or more from a recent peak, and is usually accompanied by pessimism. Both are a normal part of market cycles, and no one can reliably predict exactly when they begin or end.

## Dividends
A dividend is a portion of a company's profit paid to shareholders, usually in cash and often every quarter. Not every company pays dividends. Mature companies tend to pay them, while fast-growing companies often reinvest profits back into the business instead. Dividend payments are not guaranteed and can be reduced or cancelled.

## Market orders and limit orders
A market order buys or sells immediately at the best available price, which favors speed over price certainty. A limit order sets the maximum price you will pay when buying, or the minimum price you will accept when selling, and only executes if the market reaches that price. Limit orders give more control over price but may never be filled.

## Liquidity and volatility
Liquidity describes how easily an asset can be bought or sold without moving its price much. Heavily traded large companies are usually very liquid. Volatility describes how sharply a price moves up and down over time. Higher volatility means a wider range of possible outcomes, both good and bad.
