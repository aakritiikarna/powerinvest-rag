# Portfolio Diversification

## What diversification is
Portfolio diversification is the strategy of spreading your money across many different investments so that no single one has too much influence on your overall result. The idea behind it is often summarized as not putting all your eggs in one basket. If one holding performs badly, others may perform better and soften the impact on the whole portfolio.

## Why diversification works
Different investments respond differently to the same events. A problem at one company, such as a failed product, hurts that stock but may not affect companies in other industries. Combining assets whose prices do not move in exactly the same way can reduce the overall ups and downs of the portfolio without necessarily reducing its expected return. The key concept is correlation: assets that are not perfectly correlated provide the diversification benefit.

## Ways to diversify
Investors can diversify across asset classes, such as stocks, bonds and cash. They can diversify across sectors, such as technology, healthcare, finance, energy and consumer goods. They can diversify across company sizes, across countries and regions, and across time, for example by investing gradually. Broad index funds and ETFs offer a simple way to own many holdings at once.

## Concentration risk
A portfolio that is heavily weighted toward a single stock or a single sector has concentration risk. For example, a portfolio made up mostly of technology stocks can fall sharply if the technology sector declines, even if the individual companies are strong. Checking how your holdings are spread across sectors is a practical way to spot concentration.

## What diversification cannot do
Diversification reduces company-specific and sector-specific risk, often called unsystematic risk. It cannot remove market-wide or systematic risk, because during a broad market decline most stocks tend to fall together. It also does not guarantee a profit or protect against all losses.

## Can you over-diversify
Owning a very large number of overlapping investments can add complexity and cost without adding much protection. For example, several funds that hold the same large companies provide little extra diversification. The goal is meaningful variety among holdings, not simply a larger number of them.

## Keeping the mix on target
Over time, strong performers grow to occupy a larger share of the portfolio, which shifts its risk level. Rebalancing means periodically adjusting holdings back toward the target allocation. Many investors review their allocation once or twice a year or when it drifts beyond a set range.
